# Matching salted hashes instead of plaintext terms

| | |
|---|---|
| Author | Philip Dye |
| Date | 2026-08-16 |
| Status | draft; deliberately not started |
| Analyzed against | v0.2.0 resolution model |

## Context and scope

Every CI pattern in `../ci-term-provisioning.md` puts a private list's
plaintext on the runner for one job. That is enough when the CI provider's
secret store is trusted. It is not enough when the provider itself is outside
the trust boundary.

## Goals

Scan in CI, and do it without the plaintext of any term ever reaching the
runner.

## Non-goals

Replacing the plaintext path. Most teams trust their provider's secret store,
and for them one job's exposure is acceptable.

## The design

Store a salted hash of each term, not the term. At scan time, tokenize the
candidate text, hash each token with the same salt, and test membership.
That is the whole idea.
A term file would declare a third format beside `public` and `private`, and
`terms.py` would gain a second matching path. Resolution layering would not
change; a hashed source would take part in the union like any other.

## Alternatives considered

The three plaintext patterns in `../ci-term-provisioning.md`. They answer a
different threat: trusting the provider while keeping the exposure short.
This proposal would sit on top of them, not replace them, because of the salt
(below).

## Cross-cutting concerns

Matching has to reproduce today's behavior exactly, or change it on purpose.
The current rule is a case-insensitive `\bterm\b` regular expression, which
matches multi-word terms as literal phrases. Multi-word terms do not split
cleanly into independently hashable tokens, and `tests/test_terms.py`
exercises exactly that case.

The salt is a secret, and it does not protect much once it is known. The
terms worth hiding are short and guessable (a company name, a codename), so a
leaked salt plus a list of plausible guesses reverses a hash by trial. The
salt also has to reach the runner through one of the plaintext patterns, so
the provider this proposal distrusts can obtain it anyway.

`--show-private-terms` could no longer print a hashed term, because the term
was never read. Decision 0006's promise that an operator who asks for the
full report gets it would not hold for hashed sources.

## Verification criteria

To be written if the proposal moves to discussion. At minimum, the existing
multi-word and word-boundary tests pass unchanged against a hashed source, or
the behavior change is stated and tested.

## Open questions

How, exactly, multi-word terms are hashed without losing phrase matching.

What `--show-private-terms` prints for a hashed source.

Whether the demand exists at all. So far, no consumer has asked.
